Duties And The Lock Gate
(Read at about 130 words a minute. UK English.)

Ava and her mum were planning Trimtable, our sample booking desk for dog groomers, with a salon called The Tidy Paw in mind. The feature list was short and everyone liked it. They wanted to start building the next morning. Then Ava asked three awkward questions. Who exactly is paying? What does the law ask of an app that stores customers' details? And what is the minimum age to sign up? Nobody knew. The answer was not more features. It was a written list of duties, and one firm decision to freeze the plan.

A duty, or obligation, is something that must exist or be done before a stage or a paid build can go ahead. The factory keeps every known duty in one shared file called OBLIGATIONS.csv. The version in use, v0.6, has 107 rows. Each row says what causes the duty. It might be the build platform itself, which affects every app. It might be one of the codes on the idea card. It might be a brick, or it might be a payment provider, the company that takes card payments. A kit inherits the duties of its bricks.

At Stage 3 the app gets its own obligations ledger, section 3.7 of its document. A ledger is simply a list you keep track of. It is built from the shared file: every platform row, plus the rows for each of the app's codes, plus the rows for each brick it uses, plus the rows for each payment provider it chose. Each row then gets an owner, the gate or prompt it is due by, and a status. When a new duty is learned, from an incident or a refund, a row is added to the shared file in the same turn, so the next app inherits it.

Some rows can only be done by a person, not an agent. Here are examples from the file. Deciding the minimum age and how it is captured, which comes from the BR-LEGAL brick and is due at Lock. Deciding whether one person may hold more than one membership. Ruling on plans and prices before any plan is created. These rows are listed to the person who decides once, as one list with due points. Each then becomes a work item that blocks the gate or prompt it is due at. Agents can draft, but the decision stays with a person.

Here is the rule that gives the ledger its teeth. A gate cannot pass while a ledger row due at that gate is still open or only part done. The same goes for a paid prompt: it cannot even be presented while a row due at it is open. Two automatic checks, called CK50 and CK51, enforce this. So a duty cannot quietly slip past. If the age rule is not decided, Lock does not pass, however good the mockup looks. It sounds strict, and it is. Fixing a missed legal duty after launch costs far more.

The Lock gate sits between Stage 3 and Stage 4, and it has two firm conditions. The first is a named payer: who will pay is written down by name, not assumed. The second is a legal check for this exact scope, because a different scope can bring different law. Trimtable stores customers' names and notes about their dogs, so its SEN (0-3) code is 1, personal data. That brings data protection duties with it. The legal check reads the in and out list and the ledger together, and says whether this version can be built as planned.

Lock is always decided by a person. It never passes automatically. The engine shows one decide row with a plain question: freeze the feature list? There is a reason it is always a person. Lock includes the mockup review, and judging whether a mockup is right is not something a checklist can do. A yes freezes the list. The out list is closed, and anything on it can only come back through a new gate, never by drift. Drift is when extra features creep in a little at a time without anyone deciding.

Lock is also the moment the app is created on the build platform, with its own number and a short web name called a slug. Until now the app was a folder of notes. A live app that wants a new feature later comes back in at Lock too, as a small pack of its own, so every change gets the same check. With the feature list frozen and every duty owned, the next stage can design the tables safely. That is Stage 4, Schema. First, a quick recap of duties and the Lock gate.
