Rescue, Retire And Recycle
(Read at about 130 words a minute. UK English.)

Rory kept an eye on Stayproof, the inspection pack used by Hollin Lane Kennels. One morning the leak probe raised an incident against it. Rory's first thought was speed: fix it now, before anyone notices. His second thought was worry. Rushing a fix felt like the surest way to skip a check and break something else. Then he learned how the factory handles trouble. It keeps one build lane empty, just for rescues. The fix is fast because the way is already clear, not because any step is skipped. Let's follow Stayproof round the rescue lap.

Three gates belong to the lap rather than the road: Trouble, Back to Operate and Retire. Trouble comes first, and it is automatic. When an open incident is tagged to a stage 8 app, the engine moves that app to the Rescue room at its next refresh, within about 15 minutes. Rescue's tag is Urgent. The rule books call this a detection, not a judgement. Nobody votes on whether a leak is serious enough. The app simply leaves the healthy list, and the person who owns the factory is told straight away.

The fix re-enters the road at the Slot check, which sits inside the Invest gate. Slot allows at most 3 builds at once, with 1 lane always kept for rescue, so a rescue never waits in a queue. The fix is capped to the break. That means it repairs only what broke. Anything else the fix might touch becomes a roadmap item, a planned change for later, which comes back through the Lock gate as a small pack of its own. A rescue skips Merit and Lock, because the live app has already proved it is worth having.

Fast does not mean unchecked. A person still approves the sending of every paid build prompt, exactly as in Stage 6. The speed comes from the empty lane, never from going round the person. After the fix is sent, the result has to be proved live from outside, with the cache defeated. A cache is a saved older copy of a page that a browser or server may show instead of the newest one. Defeating it means making sure you are testing the real, current app, not a memory of yesterday's.

Once no incident is open, the engine puts one question to a person, and this is the Back to Operate gate: is the fix proved live? A yes moves Stayproof out of Rescue and back to Radar, where the daily checks carry on as before. One detail is worth knowing. A ruling says rescue should be possible from any stage, not just for live apps. That rule is still only PROPOSED. Today the engine rescues stage 8 apps only, and the course teaches what the engine does today.

Retire is the last lap gate, and the only way an app ever leaves the factory. It is asked after two losing months running, or when someone tags the app retire-ask. The person deciding looks at the app's own row: what it costs to keep, who still uses it, and when it was last opened. A yes sets the app's status to RETIRED. Customers are told and the hosting is switched off. Whether retiring also exports or erases the app's data is a ruling that has not been settled yet.

Retiring is not throwing away. Next comes Recycle, whose room is tagged Parts Back. The retired app's bricks, its kits and any proved formation go back to the Reserve room, where parts wait to be used again. A formation is a group of bricks that has been proved to work well together. The app's id, its row and its card are kept for ever. An app is never renamed or wiped from the record. Years later, someone can still see what it was, what it was built from and why it stopped.

Not every idea reaches the lap. If an idea is killed before any parts exist, for example at the Screen gate, it goes to the Recovery room, tagged Killed Or Held. Its id is called burnt, which means it is used up, yet the idea can still be brought back out later if things change. Recycle never applies at the Merit gate, because at that point there are no built parts to give back. Recovery keeps the idea and the reasons it stopped, so nobody pays to research the same dead end twice.

There is one more move, called Re-imagine. It is a door, not a stamp, meaning nobody has to approve a gate to open it. Re-imagine keeps an app's assembly, the way its parts fit together, and points it at a new audience under a new id. Stayproof's inspection pack, for instance, could be re-imagined for a different kind of animal business. A clone like that is paid work, so it goes through the Invest gate like any build. Nothing is wasted and nothing is skipped. Let's recap the lap.
