Bricks, Shelves And Kits
(Read at about 130 words a minute. UK English.)

Isla was planning Litterline, our sample licence and records hub for dog breeders. She sat down and wrote every feature she could think of: dog records, health notes, litters, reminders, forms, invoices. She wanted a full parts list she could trust. An hour later she had sixty loose items, and half of them were the same thing under different names. Bookings, appointments and reservations were all one idea. Then she saw how the factory does it. It picks a few named kits, each a known bundle of parts, and the list is clean in minutes.

A brick is one reusable part of an app that does one job. Booking is a brick. Documents is a brick. Sign on the screen is a brick. Each brick has an id that starts with BR, such as BR-BOOKING, plus a name, the tables it needs and a status. The factory keeps them all in one list called BRICK-REGISTRY, and the version in use, v0.16, holds 101 bricks. A table, by the way, is a grid of rows and columns that stores one kind of record, a bit like one tab in a spreadsheet.

Bricks sit on shelves, and the shelves are sorted on a colour spine, which is a fixed order where each shelf has its own colour. There are 8 shelves. BASICS is the floor: sign-in, menus and basic records. Boundaries holds walls, roles, security and legal. Bucks holds plans, invoices and payment rails. Body holds the parts you see, like booking and records. Brand holds the name, colours, logo and domain for each brand. Boost holds public pages, reviews and funnels. Brains holds scoring, matching and reports. Bots holds reminders, sequences and imports that run on their own.

If you count the registry, the shelves are not even. Body is by far the biggest, with 63 of the 101 bricks. That is partly because the registry folds the Bots shelf into Body: a bot is a way of doing something, not a separate thing, so bot bricks are filed under Body. After Body come Brains with 11, Bucks with 10, Boundaries with 9, Boost with 4, Brand with 3, and BASICS with just one brick, BR-BASICS. You do not need to remember the numbers. Remember that the shelf tells you what kind of job a brick does.

A kit is a named bundle of bricks that ship together, so one idea is picked as one thing with a known cost, not as fifteen loose picks. There are 9 kits, numbered K00-K08. K01 is a client portal. K02 is booking and appointments. K03 is selling a thing with a single seller. K04 is quiz to lead to report. K05 is a membership community. K06 is a breeding and livestock record. K07 is field service and job operations. K08 is own rules and checklists. Each kit names an anchor brick, the main part everything else hangs from.

K00 is different. It is called the floor, and it is forced onto every app. You cannot pick it and you cannot remove it. It holds three bricks: BR-BASICS for sign-in, menus and simple records, BR-TENANCY for keeping each business's data apart, and BR-LEGAL for consent and data protection. Between them they bring seven tables, including tenants, roles, permissions and consents. Because every kit sits on the floor, those three bricks are counted inside every kit, never added on top. K02, for example, has four bricks: the floor's three plus BR-BOOKING.

Here is how it looks for Litterline. It uses K00, the floor, like every app. Its main kit is K06, breeding and livestock record, which brings pedigree, clinical record and breeding events. A pedigree is the record of an animal's parents and ancestors. It adds K08, own rules and checklists, so a breeder can track licence conditions as rows. And it adds K01, the client portal, so puppy buyers can see their own records. Four kits, and a clear list. Trimtable, the grooming desk, picks K00, K02, K01 and K03 instead.

Kits do not stop you adding single bricks. Each kit lists optional members too, such as reminders added to booking to cut no-shows. But the order matters. Start with the floor, choose the kits that match the idea, then add any loose bricks the kits do not cover. This keeps two apps that need the same thing building it the same way, and it means a part proved in one app can be reused in the next.
