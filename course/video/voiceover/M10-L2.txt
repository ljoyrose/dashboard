Where To Go Next
(Read at about 130 words a minute. UK English.)

Leah finished the course with one idea form filled in, and she wanted to know what could come after a single idea. So she searched online, and everything she found promised apps that make money while you sleep. She trusted none of it, and she was right not to. What she needed was an honest picture: where the factory is heading, what the next steps are, and what nobody can promise. That picture turned out to be calmer and more useful. It is a road of clear steps, with a person deciding at the gates.

First, the future. Everything in the next two parts is Vision 2029, illustrative. That means it is a picture of what the factory aims to look like, drawn to show scale. It is not today's numbers and it is not a forecast. In this picture there are about 1,000 live apps, and one new app arrives each day. Every figure you hear is invented to make the idea concrete. Holding a clear picture helps the factory build the right checks now, because a check that works for one app has to work for a thousand.

Still Vision 2029, illustrative. Imagine 06:40 UK time. The leak probe reports All Clear across 18,240 tables. Three apps sit in the Rescue room, each with one capped fix moving through the lane kept empty for trouble. Two hundred and twelve apps have been retired, with their parts back in Reserve. The decide list, where a person answers yes or no, is capped at 10 rows, and 7 are open. Notice what scale does not change. The same probe, the same money line and the same gates you learned in this course run for every app.

Even in the vision, automation does the checking and the carrying between gates, and a person still decides at the human gates. Those are Screen, Merit, Lock, Invest unless it passes automatically at 1,500 credits or less, Send, Launch, Back to Operate and Retire. On the full flow map of the future factory, a person appears only at those points. That is the design, not a weakness. Machines are good at doing the same check a thousand times. People are needed where judgement, money and law meet, and that does not change with scale.

Now the next steps. A value ladder is a set of steps, where each step offers a little more help for the people who want it, and nobody has to climb higher than suits them. The App Factory ladder has five steps. One, this course, which teaches the road. Two, the idea form, where you send in your own idea. Three, an app built for you through the stages. Four, a reseller plan, where a business puts its own name on an app. Five, a partner link. Let's take them in pairs.

Step one is where you are now. The course explains how an idea becomes a live app: stages, rooms, gates, codes, bricks, walls and tests, in plain words. Step two is the idea form. A client door into the factory is being built, so the link is marked TO CONFIRM until it exists. When it opens, you would answer the same plain questions you practised with Litterline: what it is, who pays, the pain, and the required codes. A person then decides at the Screen gate whether work starts, exactly as you learned.

Step three is an app built for you, through the same stages and gates. Step four is a reseller plan. A reseller is a business that sells an app under its own name, the way the fictional Northvale Canine Society could offer Litterline to its breeders. One rule is firm: a reseller brand is never on a free plan. Members sign in first and pay second. The plan they chose on the landing page travels with them, is recorded, and is checked against that brand's offers. The price is never taken from the web address.

Step five is a partner link for the platform the factory builds on. That link is marked TO CONFIRM, because it is not set yet. And here is the most important line in this lesson. There are no income promises anywhere in this course. Results depend on your own work, and nothing here says that any app, plan or link will make money. What the course can promise is understanding: how real apps are planned, checked and run, and the questions to ask before any money is spent. Let's recap where to go next.
