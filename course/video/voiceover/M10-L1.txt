Filling In Your First Idea Form
(Read at about 130 words a minute. UK English.)

Ava had followed the whole course, and she had an app idea of her own written on a sticky note by her desk. She wanted to send it into the factory properly, the way a real idea goes in. Then she opened the form, saw the 20 codes and froze. It felt like a test she might fail. What helped was watching someone fill in a finished example first. The codes turned out to be plain questions about who signs in and what they see, and most of them allow the answer not yet known. So let's fill one in together.

Every idea enters the factory through one of four doors. The first is the owner's own idea form. Two more are internal doors for ideas the factory finds by itself, including ideas suggested by AI. The fourth is a door for clients, meaning people outside the factory, and that door is not built yet. Whichever door an idea uses, it lands in the same place: Reception, the room for Stage 0, tagged Free, because nothing is spent here. Our worked example is Litterline, a fictional app, so every name and number you hear is invented.

The form starts with a short name: Litterline. Then comes the brief, which is three plain answers. What is it? A licence and records hub for dog breeders. Who pays? Breeders, and clubs that resell it under their own name, such as the fictional Northvale Canine Society. What is the pain? Licence renewals mean weeks of hunting through paper. The form also notes the rule behind the idea, here the dog breeding licence rules in England and Wales. A good brief is short enough to say in one breath, and specific enough that a stranger could picture the customer.

Now the 20 codes, in four groups. Each code is a word plus a number. Six are required: TEN, CUST, SEN, VIEW, POST and PAY. The other 14 may say not yet known. Group 1 is Businesses. TEN (0-6) is 3, one reseller above, because the club sits above its breeders. PARENTS (0-3) is 1, so each breeder sits under one party. DOWN (0-3) is 1: the club can read the layer below. MEET (0-3) is 0, so breeders never meet each other. POOL (0-3) is 1, only when enough contribute. EXIT (0-3) is 2: a leaving breeder's data is handed to them.

Group 2 is Who Signs In. ROLE (0-2) is 2, roles with different access, so a breeder and their helper need not see the same things. BELONG (0-2) is 1: each person belongs to one business. GUEST (0-1) is 1, which means a business can let an outside person in with a scoped view, a narrow window onto only what they need. HELP (0-3) is 1: the owner can view the app as another person sees it, view only, and every look is logged. JOIN (0-2) is 1, so accounts are created through a brand address or a join link, not by anyone at all.

Group 3 is Customers. CUST (0-5) is 3, customers write, so puppy buyers can add to their records. PRICE (0-3) is 1, one price list. SHARE (0-1) is 0: members do not share with each other. SEN (0-3) is 1, personal data, but nothing health-related or about children. Group 4 is Public. VIEW (0-2) is 1, so only people holding a link can read while signed out. POST (0-2) is 0: strangers cannot put data in. BRAND (0-2) is 2, a brand per business. SEND (0-2) is 1, users download their own data. PAY (0-2) is 2: payouts within one chain.

Next come the three kill questions. FRAGILE: could it give wrong answers that look right? ONESHOT: would people use it once and stop? NOHOOK: is there no reason to come back? In our worked example all three are answered no, partly because licences renew, which brings breeders back. With no kill answer and SEN below 2, the engine suggests the verdict PASS. It also suggests a data role, such as CONTROLLER, PROCESSOR, BOTH or NONE, and a person confirms it. Then the Screen gate asks a person one question: start work on this idea?

Your turn. Take your own idea and write a short name, then the three-part brief: what it is, who pays, and the pain it removes. Answer the six required codes first, TEN, CUST, SEN, VIEW, POST and PAY, and write not yet known wherever you honestly are not sure. That is allowed, and it is better than a guess. Your answers become the app's card, its one home, which is never overwritten; changes become a new version. The link to a client idea form is marked TO CONFIRM, because that door is still being built. Let's recap.
