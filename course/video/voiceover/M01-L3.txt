Kill Check, Verdict And Data Role
(Read at about 130 words a minute. UK English.)

Leo had an idea he loved: a version of Trimtable that would make one neat printed price list for a grooming salon. He wanted it built straight away. Then it went through the kill check, and one question stopped it. Would people use it once and stop? Honestly, yes. A salon prints its price list once a year and never opens the app again. Leo was disappointed for about a minute. Then he saw the point. The idea had stopped at Stage 0, for free, before a single hour of research or a single credit was spent.

The kill check is three questions, each with a code name. FRAGILE asks: could the app give wrong answers that look right? An app that quietly misreads a record and shows it as fact is dangerous, because nobody notices. ONESHOT asks: would people use it once and stop? NOHOOK asks: is there no reason to come back? An app that nobody returns to cannot stay alive. These three can all be answered from the description alone, which is why they come first. They need no research, no tables and no money at all.

If any one of the three answers is yes, the suggested verdict is KILL. A verdict is the decision recorded for an idea at a check. A killed idea is not deleted. It is kept as evidence, with its reason and the date, and moved to the room called Recovery. That way, if someone suggests the same idea again next year, the factory finds the old record and does not repeat the work. A person still confirms a kill; the engine only suggests it. Stopping a weak idea early is one of the cheapest wins in the whole factory.

The second possible verdict is HOLD. The engine suggests HOLD when SEN, the sensitivity code, is 2 or 3. That means the app would hold health or clinical data, or data about children. These need extra care under data protection law. Before such an idea may move on, a DPIA must be done. A DPIA, or Data Protection Impact Assessment, is a written check of the risks to the people whose data the app will hold, and of how those risks will be reduced. Until it is signed, the idea waits. It is not killed, only paused.

The third verdict is PASS. An idea passes when no kill answer is yes and SEN is below 2, meaning the data is ordinary or personal, but not health and not children. Trimtable, the booking desk for groomers, is a good example. Salons use it every day to book and check appointments, so ONESHOT and NOHOOK are both no. Its records come from what the groomer types in, so FRAGILE is no. Its SEN is 1, because it holds clients' names and contact details. So the engine suggests PASS, and Trimtable moves towards the Screen gate.

Alongside the verdict, the engine suggests a data role. This comes from data protection law and says who is responsible for personal data. A CONTROLLER decides why personal data is used and how. A PROCESSOR handles personal data on behalf of another business, following its instructions. BOTH means the app is a controller for some data and a processor for other data, decided set by set. NONE means the app holds no personal data at all. The role belongs to each relationship, not the whole company, so one app can play different roles for different data.

Why does the role matter so early? Because it picks the duties. Each role brings its own pack of checklist lines that the app must complete later. A controller has duties such as a privacy notice and a route for people to ask for a copy of their data. A processor needs a written agreement with each business whose data it handles. An app marked BOTH gets both packs. Choosing the wrong role at Stage 0 means the wrong duty lines follow the app all the way to launch, so it is checked here, at the very start.

Now the idea reaches its first gate: Screen, between Stage 0 and Stage 1. The question is simple: start work on this idea? A person decides, and their yes also confirms the data role. The factory can let some ideas pass Screen automatically, but never an idea with a kill suggested, an idea seen before, or an idea from the AI or client doors. Those always wait for a person. Once Screen says yes, the idea leaves Reception and enters Research. You now know the whole of Stage 0. Let us recap the three checks.
