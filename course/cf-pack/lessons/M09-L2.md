# The Money Line
M09 · Stage 8 · Operate · Lesson 2 · about 6 min · 23 Oct 2026

**Video slot:** provider `rosequest` · id `TO CONFIRM` · type `v` · key `M09-L2`

**You can:** You can read a monthly money line and explain when the Return rule and the Retire question come in.

Sam looked after the numbers for Trimtable, the booking and client desk built for groomer Juno Platt at The Tidy Paw. Juno asked him a simple question: is the app worth keeping? Sam had pages of reports, charts of sign-ins and lists of bookings, and he still could not give her a straight answer. Every chart told a slightly different story. Then he saw how the factory does it. It asks for one line a month: what came in and what went out. That single line, written the same way every month, is enough to raise the right question at the right time.

This is called the money line. It is written on the app's card, which is the one home of the app's facts, and it follows a fixed pattern. It starts with the word money, then the year and the month, then the word in followed by the pounds that came in, then the word out followed by the pounds that went out. For example: money, September 2026, in X, out Y. Because the pattern never changes, a script can read it without guessing. Many months of lines, one under another, become a simple history of the app.

Who writes the money line? Not a script and not an AI helper. On the factory's stage 8 checklist it is tagged as the owner's own work, so a person writes it each month. That makes sense. Money facts live in bank statements, platform bills and payment reports, and a person who can see all of them is best placed to sum them honestly. The scripts then do what scripts are good at: they read the line, compare it with earlier months and raise a flag when a pattern appears. The person supplies the truth, the machine watches it.

Let's read one. These numbers are invented for teaching. Suppose Trimtable's line says in 90, out 140. More went out than came in, so that is a losing month. On its own, one losing month is information, not a verdict. Perhaps a yearly platform bill landed that month, or bookings dipped during a holiday. Nothing happens automatically after one losing month. The line simply sits on the card for anyone to see. The factory is not asking whether a single month was good. It is watching for a pattern that keeps going.

Here is the pattern it watches for. If the money line shows two losing months running, the engine asks the Retire question. Retire is a gate, and its question is: switch it off and archive it? The question appears as one row on the decide list, which is the short list of things waiting for a person's yes or no. The question can also be raised by hand, if someone tags the app with retire-ask. Asking is not deciding. A person reads the facts and chooses. This two-month rule was added on 20 Oct 2026.

There is a second money rule, called Return, ruling R02. Remember the Invest gate, where build credits are spent on paid work. Return says: no new Invest for an app until it has earned back its credits, or 90 days of use show it will. So if Juno wants a new feature for Trimtable, the factory first asks whether the app has paid back what it already cost to build. Return does not switch anything off. It guards the next spend, so money is not poured into an app that has not yet shown it can stand up.

Return is not automated yet. It is a line on the stage 8 checklist, done by an AI helper and answered with a dated note in the app's document, in the part about payoff. The rule books also tie the lap to Return, while the engine uses the two losing months rule for Retire. The course teaches both, because they do different jobs. Two losing months raises the question of switching off. Return decides whether more credits may be spent. Where the files differ, the course follows what the engine does today.

One last point matters a great deal. The money line is a record of what happened, never a forecast of what will happen. Nothing in this course promises that any app will make money, and every figure in this lesson is invented. Some apps earn back their cost and some do not, and the factory is built to notice either outcome early and honestly. That is why the line is short, monthly and written by a person. It keeps the decision clear and keeps hope out of the sums. Let's recap the money line.

## Quiz
1. What does the monthly money line record?
   - A) Sign-ins and page views
   - B) Money in and money out
   - C) The number of tables
   - D) Leak probe results
   - Answer: B. The money line records the pounds that came in and went out that month, on the app's card.
2. What happens after two losing months running?
   - A) The app is deleted at once
   - B) Prices go up by themselves
   - C) A person is asked the Retire question
   - D) The app moves to Research
   - Answer: C. The engine asks a person whether to switch the app off and archive it; asking is not deciding.
3. What does the Return rule R02 say?
   - A) No new Invest until the app earns back its credits, or 90 days of use show it will
   - B) Every app must make money in its first week
   - C) Apps return to stage 0 each year
   - D) Refunds are never given
   - Answer: A. Return guards the next spend: no new Invest until the credits are earned back or 90 days show they will be.
4. Who writes the monthly money line?
   - A) The leak probe
   - B) A visitor to the sales page
   - C) The building platform
   - D) A person, as named work on the checklist
   - Answer: D. The money line is tagged as the owner's own work, so a person writes it and scripts read it.
