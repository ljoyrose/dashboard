# The Road, Rooms And Gates
M00 · What Is An App Factory · Lesson 2 · about 6 min · 23 Oct 2026

**Video slot:** provider `rosequest` · id `TO CONFIRM` · type `v` · key `M00-L2`

**You can:** You can name the 9 stages, describe the 11 rooms and say which gates need a person to decide.

Sam is fifteen. One weekend he had an idea for a small app to help his gran keep track of her dog's walks and feeding times, and he wanted it finished by Sunday night. So he skipped straight to building. By Sunday it worked, until his cousin signed in and could read his gran's private notes. Sam had wanted speed. What stopped him was a mistake he never checked for. The lesson he took away was simple: an app is safer when it travels a fixed road, with gates where it must stop and prove itself before it moves on.

In the factory, every idea travels the same road of nine stages, numbered 0 to 8. Stage 0 is Idea, where the idea is written down. Stage 1 is Research, where we check whether people need it. Stage 2 is Base, where we pick a starting point. Stage 3 is Features, where we choose what goes in. Stage 4 is Schema, the plan of the tables. Stage 5 is Data, where test accounts are set up. Stage 6 is Prompts, where the app is actually built. Stage 7 is Launch. Stage 8 is Operate, where the live app is watched every day.

Two things make this road easy to follow. First, the money. Stages 0 to 5 cost no build credits at all. Build credits are what the building platform charges each time it writes code. Stage 6, Prompts, is the only stage that spends them, so every check before it is free. Second, the paperwork. Each app has a card, which holds its 20 codes and is never overwritten, and one document organised by stage. Each stage of that document ends with a line saying when it is finished. That line is the test for the stage.

The road runs through rooms. A room is simply where an app sits while it is at a certain stage, so anyone can see at a glance what is happening. There are 11 rooms in all. Six sit on the main road. Reception holds new ideas at Stage 0. Research holds Stages 1, 2 and 3. The Rig holds Stages 4 and 5, where tables and test data are set up. The Reactor holds Stage 6, the paid build. The Runway holds Stage 7, launch. And Radar holds Stage 8, where live apps are watched.

The other five rooms sit beside the road. Recovery holds ideas that were killed or held, so they are kept as evidence and can be looked at again. Reserve holds spare parts, such as bricks and kits from apps that were switched off. The Ready Room is where locked jobs wait for a free building lane. Rescue is for live apps in trouble that need an urgent fix. And Recycle is where a retired app's parts go back onto the shelves. Nothing in the factory is simply thrown away. Even a failure teaches the next app something.

Between the stages sit gates. A gate is a stop point where the app must prove its stage is finished before it may move on. There are 8 gates on the main road. Screen sits between Stages 0 and 1. Merit sits between 1 and 2. Base sits between 2 and 3. Lock sits between 3 and 4. Schema sits between 4 and 5. Invest sits between 5 and 6. Send sits between 6 and 7. Launch sits between 7 and 8. A gate cannot open while any checklist line for its stage is still not done.

Some gates open by themselves when every check passes. Base and Schema are automatic. Invest is automatic when the build is estimated at 1,500 credits or less. At the others, a person decides: Screen, Merit, Lock, Invest when the build is bigger, Send and Launch. Their yes is the only thing that moves the app. Inside some gates sit smaller checks. Slot, inside Invest, makes sure no more than 3 apps are being built at once, with 1 lane kept free for rescues. Leak and Live, inside Launch, prove nothing leaks and the first real sign-in works.

Launch is not the end. A live app goes round a lap, and three lap gates watch it. Trouble is automatic: if an incident is opened on a live app, the app moves to Rescue. Back to Operate is a person's decision, and the question is whether the fix is proved live. Retire is also a person's decision: should the app be switched off and archived? So that is the whole map. Nine stages, 11 rooms, 8 road gates and 3 lap gates, with a person deciding at the moments that matter most. Let us recap before Stage 0.

## Recap
- Nine Stages Run From 0 Idea To 8 Operate
- Eleven Rooms Show Where Each App Sits
- Gates Stop Apps, A Person Decides At Some
- **Why It Matters:** A Fixed Road Catches Mistakes Before Real People Do

## Quiz
1. How many stages are on the factory road?
   - A) Seven
   - B) Eight
   - C) Nine, numbered 0 to 8
   - D) Eleven
   - Answer: C. The road has nine stages, from 0 Idea to 8 Operate.
2. Which is the only stage that spends build credits?
   - A) Stage 1, Research
   - B) Stage 6, Prompts
   - C) Stage 4, Schema
   - D) Stage 8, Operate
   - Answer: B. Stage 6 is where the app is built, and it is the only stage that spends build credits.
3. Which of these gates opens by itself when every check passes?
   - A) Lock
   - B) Launch
   - C) Merit
   - D) Schema
   - Answer: D. Schema is an automatic gate, like Base; Lock and Launch always need a person.
4. What does the Slot check inside Invest make sure of?
   - A) No more than 3 builds at once, with 1 lane kept for rescues
   - B) The app has a logo
   - C) The price is set
   - D) Every screen has a colour
   - Answer: A. Slot limits building to 3 apps at once and keeps 1 lane free for rescues.
