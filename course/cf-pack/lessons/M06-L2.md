# The Leak And Tenant Tests
M06 · Stage 5 · Data · Lesson 2 · about 6 min · 23 Oct 2026

**Video slot:** provider `rosequest` · id `TO CONFIRM` · type `v` · key `M06-L2`

**You can:** You can describe a test plan with negative cases, read a test matrix and explain what the Invest gate decides.

Isla is sixteen. She wrote a checklist to test Stayproof, the inspection pack for boarding kennels, using Hollin Lane Kennels as her example. She wanted proof that each kennel's inspection logs stayed private. Her checklist said things like: a kennel worker can open the daily log, can add a cleaning note, can print the pack. Every line passed. She felt good, until someone asked a simple question. Did you test whether a different kennel can open Hollin Lane's logs? She had not. Her list only tested what should work. The tests that matter most check what must never happen.

In Stage 5 the factory writes a test plan. The rule is one row per test, with its negative case. A positive case checks that something allowed works: a Brand A member can read Brand A's dogs. A negative case checks that something forbidden fails: a Brand B member tries to read Brand A's dogs and gets nothing. Both halves matter. An app that blocks everyone passes every negative case and is useless. An app that lets everyone in passes every positive case and is dangerous. The pair proves the wall is in the right place.

The tests are laid out as a test matrix. A matrix is a grid. Down the side goes every table in the app. Across the top go four kinds of visitor: someone signed out, a Brand A member, a Brand B member, and the owner. Each square says what that visitor should get from that table: everything, their own rows only, or nothing at all. Every table is tested, not only the tables a screen happens to use. Old or forgotten tables are exactly where leaks hide, because nobody is looking at them.

A leak is when someone can see or change data that belongs to somebody else. Brand B reading Brand A's dogs is a leak. A signed-out stranger reading a customer list is a leak. The factory treats a leak as a stop, not as a bug to fix later. The gate rules say leaks are watched throughout the build, and any leak stops the build. Each app carries a leak status on its card. L with a question mark means not tested. Ln means tested, with no leak. Ly means a leak was found.

Here is the clever part. The leak tests are written now, in Stage 5, together with the build prompts, before a single credit is spent. A prompt is the written instruction given to the AI builder. The factory also writes scenario tests in plain prose, which describe a real person's journey step by step, and splits the full prompt sequence into parts. When the paid stage starts, each prompt already has its tests waiting. Nobody has to invent a test after seeing the result, when it is tempting to test only what looks right.

Next, money. The factory estimates how many credits the build will take and writes it on the app's card as the word credits, then a number. The factory owner also sets a credit ceiling, the most this app may spend, and a stop rule. The template gives a default ceiling of 10,000 credits, and says stop at one and a half times the estimate, after two failures, or at 80 percent of the ceiling. A rollback point is saved too: an export ZIP, which is a packed copy of the app's files, with any gaps noted.

Stage 5 ends at the Invest gate, from 5 to 6. Its question is: spend build credits? If the estimate is 1,500 credits or less, and the month's stop line has not been passed, Invest passes by itself. Otherwise the factory owner decides, because this is where real money starts to be spent. Inside Invest sits the Slot check. The factory runs at most three builds at once and always keeps one lane empty for Rescue, for a live app that breaks. If the lanes are full, the app simply waits its turn.

So when Stage 5 closes, the app has two contrasting test brands, named test members, a test plan with a negative case on every row, and a test matrix covering every table for four kinds of visitor. It has a credit estimate, a ceiling, a stop rule and a rollback point. Nothing has been paid for yet. Isla's lesson is the factory's lesson: test what must never happen, write the tests first, and treat any leak as a stop. Next, in Stage 6, the prompts are finally sent to the builder.

## Recap
- Every Test Row Carries Its Negative Case
- Matrix Covers Every Table For Four Kinds Of Visitor
- Invest Passes Itself At 1,500 Credits Or Less
- **Why It Matters:** Tests Written First Cannot Bend To Fit Results

## Quiz
1. What is a negative case?
   - A) A test that always passes
   - B) A test of the sales page wording
   - C) A test that checks something forbidden really fails
   - D) A test that is only run on a phone
   - Answer: C. A negative case proves that something which must never happen, such as Brand B reading Brand A's rows, really does fail.
2. Which four kinds of visitor does the test matrix cover?
   - A) Owner, reseller, customer and builder
   - B) Signed out, Brand A member, Brand B member and owner
   - C) Desktop, phone, tablet and laptop
   - D) Breeder, groomer, kennel and club
   - Answer: B. Every table is checked for a signed-out visitor, a member of each test brand and the owner.
3. What happens when a leak is found during the build?
   - A) It is noted for a later version
   - B) Only the owner is told
   - C) The leak status stays as not tested
   - D) The build stops
   - Answer: D. The gate rules say leaks are watched throughout the build and any leak stops the build.
4. When does the Invest gate pass by itself?
   - A) When the estimate is 1,500 credits or less and the month's stop line is not passed
   - B) Whenever the test plan is written
   - C) When two test members exist
   - D) Never, the owner always decides
   - Answer: A. Invest auto-passes at 1,500 credits or less within the month's stop line; above that, the factory owner decides.
