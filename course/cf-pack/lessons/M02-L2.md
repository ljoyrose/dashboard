# The Merit Gate
M02 · Stage 1 · Research · Lesson 2 · about 6 min · 23 Oct 2026

**Video slot:** provider `rosequest` · id `TO CONFIRM` · type `v` · key `M02-L2`

**You can:** You can explain the four Merit verdicts as a decision tree, say why a person decides and describe the DPIA hold.

Ruby is fourteen and had followed Litterline all the way through research. The rivals were checked, the customer words collected, and the gap matrix was clear. She wanted a quick yes so the building could start. Instead, the verdict came back RESHAPE, with one line: build it for clubs to resell from the very start, not as an extra later. Ruby was annoyed, until she read the reason and saw that it was right. A clear instruction to change one thing was worth far more than a vague yes that would have caused trouble later.

Merit is the gate between Stage 1, Research, and Stage 2, Base. Its question is: is it worth building? Everything from the research sweep is laid in front of the person who decides: the rivals, the customer words, the gap matrix, the market size, the rules flag and the rank. Before Merit can even be asked, every research line must be done, and the research section of the app's document must not say pending. Merit is the last gate before real work on the app begins, so it is the last cheap place to stop.

Why does a person decide here, and not a tool? Because Merit is the regulatory and data protection kill. It asks whether the law, or the way the app handles people's data, makes the idea unsafe or unwise to build. In this factory the person at Merit is trained in law, and the rule books say plainly that no tool substitutes. For an app used by service businesses, the key question is whose data it holds. The engine has a setting that could let Merit pass on its own, but the rule books treat it as a person's decision.

Merit gives one of four verdicts: GO, HOLD, KILL or RESHAPE. They are not a list to pick from at random. They work as a decision tree, which means a short chain of questions asked in order. If the idea is good as it is, the verdict is GO. If not, ask: can you name what to change? If yes, the verdict is RESHAPE. If not, ask: can you name what to wait for? If yes, the verdict is HOLD. If you can name neither, the verdict is KILL. Every verdict is recorded with a date and a reason.

RESHAPE means the idea is worth something, but one part must change first. The idea keeps its id, which is its permanent number, and goes back to Reception to be reworked. Ruby's Litterline verdict was exactly this. HOLD means the idea is fine, but something outside it must happen first, and the verdict names that trigger, such as waiting for a new rule to be published. Simply waiting for a free building lane is not a HOLD. That is a queue, and a queue is not a verdict. Queues are handled later, at the Slot check.

KILL means the idea stops. It burns the id, which means that number is never handed to another idea. At Merit, no parts have been built yet, so a killed idea goes to the room called Recovery, with its reason kept as evidence. It can be looked at again later if something changes. There is another verdict, called RECYCLE, used when an app's parts go back on the shelves, but RECYCLE never applies at Merit. There are no parts to recycle yet. That is part of why stopping here costs so little.

There is one special hold to know about. If SEN, the sensitivity code, is 2 or 3, the app would hold health data or children's data. Such an idea is held until its DPIA, the written risk check, is done and signed, and the card stays on HOLD until then. The factory saves effort here: a base DPIA is written once for the shared core, and each new app built from it adds a one-page add-on. Some rules come with it, such as photos being private by default and children's details never being public.

When the verdict is GO, it must be carried. A verdict given on a screen is not enough. The rule is simple: a move writes, or it did not happen. In the same session, the verdict is written onto the card and into the app register, the list of every app. The app's folder is copied from the factory template, and the idea leaves the board of decisions waiting. Now it is a real app at Stage 2, Base. Litterline went back, was reshaped for clubs, and later came through Merit with a GO. Let us recap.

## Recap
- Merit Asks: Is It Worth Building
- GO, HOLD, KILL Or RESHAPE, As A Decision Tree
- SEN (0-3) At 2 Or More Needs A DPIA
- **Why It Matters:** A Person Judges The Law Before Anything Is Built

## Quiz
1. What question does the Merit gate ask?
   - A) Start work on this idea?
   - B) Freeze the feature list?
   - C) Is it worth building?
   - D) Open it to real users?
   - Answer: C. Merit asks whether the idea is worth building, after the research is done.
2. At Merit you can name exactly what must change. Which verdict fits?
   - A) GO
   - B) KILL
   - C) HOLD
   - D) RESHAPE
   - Answer: D. If you can name what to change, the verdict is RESHAPE and the idea goes back to Reception.
3. Why does RECYCLE never apply at Merit?
   - A) No parts have been built yet
   - B) Merit is automatic
   - C) RECYCLE costs too much
   - D) The card is deleted at Merit
   - Answer: A. RECYCLE returns parts to the shelves, and at Merit no parts exist yet.
4. What does the rule "a move writes, or it did not happen" mean?
   - A) Every app needs a writer
   - B) The verdict must be written to the card and register in the same session
   - C) The idea must be written twice
   - D) Only written ideas can enter Reception
   - Answer: B. A verdict only counts once it is written into the card and the app register in the same session.
