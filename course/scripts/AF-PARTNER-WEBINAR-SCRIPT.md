## Slide 1 · Title
**Beat:** Title Hook · 63 words · about 29 s

Welcome. In the next twenty minutes I'll show you how an app gets made in a factory, not in a back bedroom. You'll see the road every idea travels, the checks that keep your customers' data safe, and how your business can start with one short form. Everything you see uses invented businesses and invented numbers, so nothing here is anyone's private data.

## Slide 2 · Where This Started
**Beat:** Origin Story · Epiphany Bridge: Backstory, Desire, Wall · 126 words · about 58 s

Let me tell you where this started. And a note for whoever presents this: this story is to be checked with Lisa before use. I run animal businesses, and I trained as a solicitor, so rules and records are part of my everyday life. I wanted simple tools for my own clients. Every app I paid for was hand-made from nothing. Each one had different gaps. When I asked a simple question, 'can one customer ever see another customer's records?', nobody could give me a straight answer with proof. And none of those apps learned anything from the one before. That was my wall. I had good ideas, and I had customers who wanted them, but I had fragile apps and no way to trust them.

## Slide 3 · The Moment It Changed
**Beat:** Origin Story · Epiphany Bridge: Epiphany, Plan · 118 words · about 54 s

Then it clicked. Car makers don't hand-carve every car. They have a production line, standard parts and an inspection at every station, and every car that rolls off the line is better because of the ones before it. So I stopped trying to build apps, and I built the factory that builds them. One road with nine stages. A shelf of proven parts that we call bricks. And a gate between every stage, where the work is checked before it's allowed to move on. Each new app reuses what the last one proved. When something goes wrong, the fix becomes a new line on the checklist, so it can't happen again. That's the plan you're about to see.

## Slide 4 · The One Thing
**Beat:** The One Thing · 117 words · about 54 s

If you remember one slide from today, make it this one. A factory with gates is what makes a custom app safe, fast and affordable. Here's why. A problem caught at the idea stage costs a short conversation. The same problem caught while building costs credits and time. Caught after launch, it costs customers, money and trust, and sometimes a call from a regulator. So the factory refuses to move an app forward until the evidence for that stage is there. Every stage has a finished-when line, and every gate checks it. That discipline is what lets one small team ship many apps without cutting corners, and it's what lets you trust the app with your customers.

## Slide 5 · Three Things Most People Get Wrong
**Beat:** 3 Secrets Overview · 115 words · about 53 s

Now, there are three beliefs that stop business owners from getting the app they need, and I want to break each one. The first is about the vehicle: the belief that every app has to be invented from nothing, so it must be slow and expensive. The second is about you: the belief that you need to be technical, or hire someone technical, to get an app made. The third is about the world outside: the belief that safety, privacy and the law are someone else's problem, to be sorted out later. Each of the next sections takes one of those beliefs and shows you what the factory does instead. Let's start with the vehicle.

## Slide 6 · Secret 1 · Eight Shelves Of Bricks
**Beat:** Secret 1 · Vehicle: Teach · 132 words · about 61 s

Secret one. Apps are assembled, not invented. Every app is built from bricks: small, proven parts, each one already tested in other apps. There are one hundred and one bricks in the register today, and they sit on eight shelves. BASICS is the floor that every app gets: sign-in and menus. Boundaries holds the walls between businesses, customers and the public. Bucks handles plans and payments. Body is everything your customers actually see, like bookings and records. Brand swaps the name, colours and logo. Boost gets you found, with public pages and reviews. Brains works things out, like scores and reports. And Bots run on their own, sending reminders and doing imports. Bricks then group into kits, numbered K00 to K08: a booking kit, a client portal kit, a breeding records kit.

## Slide 7 · Secret 2 · You Just Fill In A Form
**Beat:** Secret 2 · Internal: Teach · 134 words · about 62 s

Secret two. You don't need to be technical; you need to be clear. Your part starts with one form. It asks for your vision in a sentence, who pays, and the pain you solve for them. Then it asks twenty short code questions, in plain words. For example: can a stranger read anything without signing in? Can one person belong to two businesses? Six of those questions must be answered. The rest are allowed to say 'not yet known', and the factory will find out. Then come three kill questions: could it give wrong answers that look right, would people use it once and stop, and is there no reason to come back. Finally the factory suggests a verdict, pass, hold or kill, and the data role: who is responsible for the personal data.

## Slide 8 · Secret 3 · Walls Keep Data Apart
**Beat:** Secret 3 · External: Teach · 118 words · about 54 s

Secret three. Safety and the law are built in, not bolted on at the end. The factory has eight walls, and each app gets the ones its codes call for. The brand wall means one business can never see another business's data, even inside the same app. The member wall means one customer can never see another customer's records. The public wall controls exactly what a signed-out stranger can read or submit. And the sensitivity hold stops any app that handles health or children's data until a proper data protection impact check has been done and signed. These walls aren't promises in a brochure. As you're about to see, they're tested, again and again, from outside the app.

## Slide 9 · Where This Leads
**Beat:** Value Ladder · 118 words · about 54 s

There are several ways in, and you can start wherever fits. You can learn how it all works with this course. You can send one idea through the idea form and get an honest verdict. You can have your app built through the factory. If you have an audience of your own, like members, clients or a club, you can put your brand on apps for them with a reseller plan. And if you simply know businesses that need this, you can share a partner link. Each step leads naturally to the next, but none of them forces it. The best place for most people to start is the idea form, because it costs nothing to fill in.
