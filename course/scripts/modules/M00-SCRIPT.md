## M00-L1 · What An App Is Made Of
**Outcome:** You can name the four things every app is made of and explain why a factory reuses tested parts.  
**Length:** 796 words · about 6.1 min · Today

**Opener (Epiphany Bridge (light)):** Backstory: Ava helps on Saturdays at The Tidy Paw, a grooming salon where bookings live on paper cards. · Desire: She wanted to build the groomer, Juno Platt, an app that would stop clients missing appointments. · Wall: She opened an app builder, faced a blank page and had no idea where to begin. · Epiphany: Every app is made of the same four things, screens, tables, rules and people, and you can name them before building.

| # | On Screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ava, A Blank Page And Four Things | Ava is thirteen and helps on Saturdays at The Tidy Paw, a dog grooming salon run by Juno Platt. Juno keeps her bookings on paper cards, and every week a few clients simply do not turn up. Ava wanted to build Juno an app that would fix this. She opened an app builder, stared at the blank page, and stopped. She did not know where to begin. Then she noticed something that changed how she saw it: every app, however big, is made of the same four things. Screens, tables, rules and people. Name those four first, and the blank page stops being blank. |
| 2 | Screens Are What People See | Start with screens. A screen is one page of an app that a person looks at and touches, such as a calendar, a form or a list. For Juno's salon, one screen might show today's bookings. Another might let a client pick a time. A screen does not store anything on its own. It only shows information and collects it. That matters, because a lot of people think the screens are the app. They are only the front window. Behind every screen sits the part that remembers things, and that is where we go next. |
| 3 | Tables Hold The Data In Rows | Behind the screens sit tables. A table is where an app stores its data, laid out in rows and columns, rather like a spreadsheet. Each row is one thing, such as one booking. Each column is one fact about it, such as the date, the time or the dog's name. Juno's salon would need a table of bookings, a table of clients and a table of free time slots. The plan of all the tables an app needs is called a schema. Get the tables right and the screens are easy to change later. Get them wrong and everything wobbles. |
| 4 | Rules Decide Who Sees What | Next come rules. A rule decides who may see something and who may change it. Juno should see all her own clients. A client should see only their own bookings, never anyone else's. If a second salon used the same app, neither salon should ever see the other's data. In this course, a rule that keeps one group's data apart from another's is called a wall. When a wall fails and someone sees data that is not theirs, that is called a leak. A leak is the most serious mistake an app can make, so the factory tests for leaks again and again. |
| 5 | People: Who Uses The App | The fourth thing is people. Every app has different kinds of people using it, and each kind needs different screens and different rules. There is the owner who runs the app. There are the businesses that use it, like Juno's salon. There are those businesses' customers, like the dog owners who book a trim. And there is the public: strangers who have not signed in at all. These ideas come back in the four groups of codes you will meet in Stage 0. Knowing exactly who signs in, and who does not, shapes every table and every rule. |
| 6 | Most Apps Are Built By Hand | So why does this matter? Most apps today are made by hand. Someone starts from a blank page, invents the screens, the tables and the rules from nothing, and makes hundreds of choices along the way. The next app starts from a blank page again. The same mistakes get made twice, and the same safety checks get forgotten twice. Nothing learned on one app is carried to the next. That can work for a single app. It does not work if you want many apps that are all safe, all tested and all built the same careful way. |
| 7 | A Factory Reuses Tested Parts | An app factory takes a different route. Every idea travels the same road, in the same order, with the same checklist at each step. Instead of inventing parts from nothing, the factory reuses tested parts called bricks. There are 101 bricks, kept on 8 shelves, and they are grouped into 9 ready-made bundles called kits, numbered K00-K08. Kit K00 is the floor that every app gets: the basics, the walls between businesses, and the legal and consent parts. When a problem is found on one app, the fix becomes a checklist line, so the next app does not repeat it. |
| 8 | Ava's Plan Before Building | Let us put it together. Ava now has a plan before she builds anything. For Juno's salon she can list the screens: today's bookings and a booking page. She can list the tables: bookings, clients and time slots. She can write the rules: each client sees only their own bookings, and each salon sees only its own clients. And she can name the people: Juno, her clients and the public. That list is the start of every app the factory makes. Next you will see the road an idea travels, its rooms and its gates. First, a recap. |

**Recap**
- Screens Show And Collect Information
- Tables Store Data In Rows And Columns
- Rules And Walls Decide Who Sees What
- Why it matters: Name The Four Parts Before You Build Anything

**Quiz**
1. What is a table in an app? A) A page that people tap · B) Where data is stored in rows and columns · C) A rule about prices · D) A kind of screen for staff · **Answer B**: A table stores the app's data, with one row per thing and one column per fact.
2. In this course, what is a leak? A) A slow screen · B) A missing booking · C) Someone seeing data that is not theirs · D) A spelling mistake on a page · **Answer C**: A leak happens when a wall fails and a person sees data that belongs to someone else.
3. What is kit K00? A) The floor that every app gets · B) A kit for selling products · C) A kit only for big apps · D) The last kit to be added · **Answer A**: K00 is the floor: the basics, the walls between businesses and the legal parts, on every app.
4. Why is building every app by hand a problem when you want many apps? A) Hand-made apps cannot have screens · B) Hand-made apps have no tables · C) Hand-made apps are always free · D) Lessons from one app are not carried to the next · **Answer D**: Each hand-made app starts from a blank page, so the same mistakes and missed checks repeat.

## M00-L2 · The Road, Rooms And Gates
**Outcome:** You can name the 9 stages, describe the 11 rooms and say which gates need a person to decide.  
**Length:** 783 words · about 6.0 min · Today

**Opener (Epiphany Bridge (light)):** Backstory: Sam built a small app for his gran in one weekend, skipping every check. · Desire: He wanted it finished fast, by Sunday night. · Wall: When his cousin signed in, she could read his gran's private notes. · Epiphany: An app is safer when it travels a fixed road, with gates where it must stop and prove itself.

| # | On Screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sam Wanted It Done By Sunday | Sam is fifteen. One weekend he had an idea for a small app to help his gran keep track of her dog's walks and feeding times, and he wanted it finished by Sunday night. So he skipped straight to building. By Sunday it worked, until his cousin signed in and could read his gran's private notes. Sam had wanted speed. What stopped him was a mistake he never checked for. The lesson he took away was simple: an app is safer when it travels a fixed road, with gates where it must stop and prove itself before it moves on. |
| 2 | Nine Stages, Numbered 0 To 8 | In the factory, every idea travels the same road of nine stages, numbered 0 to 8. Stage 0 is Idea, where the idea is written down. Stage 1 is Research, where we check whether people need it. Stage 2 is Base, where we pick a starting point. Stage 3 is Features, where we choose what goes in. Stage 4 is Schema, the plan of the tables. Stage 5 is Data, where test accounts are set up. Stage 6 is Prompts, where the app is actually built. Stage 7 is Launch. Stage 8 is Operate, where the live app is watched every day. |
| 3 | Free Stages, One Card, One Document | Two things make this road easy to follow. First, the money. Stages 0 to 5 cost no build credits at all. Build credits are what the building platform charges each time it writes code. Stage 6, Prompts, is the only stage that spends them, so every check before it is free. Second, the paperwork. Each app has a card, which holds its 20 codes and is never overwritten, and one document organised by stage. Each stage of that document ends with a line saying when it is finished. That line is the test for the stage. |
| 4 | Six Rooms On The Main Road | The road runs through rooms. A room is simply where an app sits while it is at a certain stage, so anyone can see at a glance what is happening. There are 11 rooms in all. Six sit on the main road. Reception holds new ideas at Stage 0. Research holds Stages 1, 2 and 3. The Rig holds Stages 4 and 5, where tables and test data are set up. The Reactor holds Stage 6, the paid build. The Runway holds Stage 7, launch. And Radar holds Stage 8, where live apps are watched. |
| 5 | Five Rooms Beside The Road | The other five rooms sit beside the road. Recovery holds ideas that were killed or held, so they are kept as evidence and can be looked at again. Reserve holds spare parts, such as bricks and kits from apps that were switched off. The Ready Room is where locked jobs wait for a free building lane. Rescue is for live apps in trouble that need an urgent fix. And Recycle is where a retired app's parts go back onto the shelves. Nothing in the factory is simply thrown away. Even a failure teaches the next app something. |
| 6 | Eight Gates On The Main Road | Between the stages sit gates. A gate is a stop point where the app must prove its stage is finished before it may move on. There are 8 gates on the main road. Screen sits between Stages 0 and 1. Merit sits between 1 and 2. Base sits between 2 and 3. Lock sits between 3 and 4. Schema sits between 4 and 5. Invest sits between 5 and 6. Send sits between 6 and 7. Launch sits between 7 and 8. A gate cannot open while any checklist line for its stage is still not done. |
| 7 | A Person Decides At Some Gates | Some gates open by themselves when every check passes. Base and Schema are automatic. Invest is automatic when the build is estimated at 1,500 credits or less. At the others, a person decides: Screen, Merit, Lock, Invest when the build is bigger, Send and Launch. Their yes is the only thing that moves the app. Inside some gates sit smaller checks. Slot, inside Invest, makes sure no more than 3 apps are being built at once, with 1 lane kept free for rescues. Leak and Live, inside Launch, prove nothing leaks and the first real sign-in works. |
| 8 | After Launch, Three Lap Gates | Launch is not the end. A live app goes round a lap, and three lap gates watch it. Trouble is automatic: if an incident is opened on a live app, the app moves to Rescue. Back to Operate is a person's decision, and the question is whether the fix is proved live. Retire is also a person's decision: should the app be switched off and archived? So that is the whole map. Nine stages, 11 rooms, 8 road gates and 3 lap gates, with a person deciding at the moments that matter most. Let us recap before Stage 0. |

**Recap**
- Nine Stages Run From 0 Idea To 8 Operate
- Eleven Rooms Show Where Each App Sits
- Gates Stop Apps, A Person Decides At Some
- Why it matters: A Fixed Road Catches Mistakes Before Real People Do

**Quiz**
1. How many stages are on the factory road? A) Seven · B) Eight · C) Nine, numbered 0 to 8 · D) Eleven · **Answer C**: The road has nine stages, from 0 Idea to 8 Operate.
2. Which is the only stage that spends build credits? A) Stage 1, Research · B) Stage 6, Prompts · C) Stage 4, Schema · D) Stage 8, Operate · **Answer B**: Stage 6 is where the app is built, and it is the only stage that spends build credits.
3. Which of these gates opens by itself when every check passes? A) Lock · B) Launch · C) Merit · D) Schema · **Answer D**: Schema is an automatic gate, like Base; Lock and Launch always need a person.
4. What does the Slot check inside Invest make sure of? A) No more than 3 builds at once, with 1 lane kept for rescues · B) The app has a logo · C) The price is set · D) Every screen has a colour · **Answer A**: Slot limits building to 3 apps at once and keeps 1 lane free for rescues.
